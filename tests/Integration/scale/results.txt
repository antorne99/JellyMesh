# What is left

Every unscanned phase at 3 x 55,000 takes about twice its linear projection from 3 x 16,500: first 267.6 s against
130 s, incremental 65.7 s against 31 s, repeat 163.0 s against 86 s, nightly 840.4 s against 315 s. The cause of
this growth is not identified. What was measured, partly on a separate 3 x 55,000 run profiled with stack samples
every 5 s (3 s in the incremental) and dotnet-counters, which slowed it:
- GC pauses are about 5% of the time (11.8 s of the 224 s first sync, 7.1 s of the 95 s incremental). The process
  uses 1.5 to 1.8 cores, mostly in user mode: 268 of 399 CPU seconds in the first sync (131 in the kernel, the file
  writes), 132 of 141 in the incremental.
- The catalog read grows with what is already indexed: in the first sync the three servers, with identical catalogs,
  took 9.1, 20.2 and 24.0 s (31, 70 and 83 ms a page), against 1.8, 1.9 and 2.0 s at 3 x 16,500.
- First sync: of the 30 samples, 9 had the sync thread in LibraryMaterializer.WriteFileAsync (open, rename, unlink).
  It writes 529,500 files (165,000 .strm, 174,000 .nfo, 190,500 images) where one server of 55,000 wrote 171,500 in
  36 s. Two samples were in CredentialVault.GetKeyState, which decrypts the credentials for each remote call.
- Sync without changes: no helper holds most samples. They are spread over the full re-plan of 165,000 stubs: the
  resolver and dedup, the planner (an .nfo rendered per stub to compare its hash, the series lookups, the groups), and
  the two saves of the 169 MiB registry with their lookups rebuilt.
- In 6 of the 12 incremental samples a request thread was in CatalogSyncService.ToReport, the status this harness polls
  every 2 s, which counts each server's items by kind on every call. Whether it slows the sync is not verified.
- Scanned version links: Run.FindStub -> LibraryManager.GetItemById -> BaseItemRepository.RetrieveItem, one SQLite
  query per stub that has an item, about 0.36 ms each (8 of 12 samples at 3 x 16,500). The per-stub cost is the same
  at 3 x 5,500 and 3 x 16,500, and on the run that links and the run after it: had the batched id check missed the
  alternates Jellyfin hides behind their primary, the second run would have looked up a third of the stubs.
- Memory: process VmHWM 4.1 GiB after the first sync, 5.7 GiB after the repeat, 6.7 GiB after the nightly, cgroup
  peak 6.9 GiB of the 7.6 GiB VM; 2.6 GiB after the nightly at 3 x 16,500. federation-index.json is 549 MiB and
  federated-stubs.json 169 MiB at 3 x 55,000.
- The scan of 3 x 16,500 linked folders took 1346 s (27 ms an item). A scanned 3 x 55,000 run would need about 75 min
  of scan on top of 6.7 GiB already reached unscanned, so it was not run; 3 x 16,500 is the largest scanned size.
