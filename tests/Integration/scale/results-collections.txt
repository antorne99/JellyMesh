# Row 232: what it comes to

At 10,000 films and 1,000 collections (7,490 member films), measured with collections-cost.sh (raw output below):
- A page of Provider/Collections takes 0.22-0.25 s (median, first, middle and last page alike; slowest single page
  0.27 s; the first request after a restart 0.44 s), against the consumer's 30 s timeout. The full walk of 15
  pages takes 4.3 s (3.6 s in the server) and 4.0 CPU-seconds, with the resident set at 550-590 MiB against 442 MiB idle.
- For comparison, a page of Provider/Items takes 0.21-0.23 s and its full walk of 20 pages 6.2 s (4.7 s in the
  server), 4.9 CPU-seconds, with 1.28 MB per page against 30 KB for Collections.
- Most of a Collections page is fixed: limit=1 takes 0.23 s against 0.03 s on Items. That fixed part is what each page
  rebuilds and filters by (every collection and its members); it grew from 0.05 s at 1,946 members (250 collections)
  to 0.20 s at 7,490, about 27 us per member, so the walk grows with the square of the member films.
- Four walks at once (one peer login, standing in for four consumers): 9.0 s for the four Collections walks
  (28 CPU-seconds), 17 s for four Items walks (46 CPU-seconds), with the process peaking at 661 MiB. Running at once
  roughly doubles the time and CPU of each walk on both routes alike.

Projected, not measured, from 27 us per member and pages of 500, at the same 7.5 films per collection (both
checkpoints had it, so a cost per collection, about 200 us, fits as well): 20,000 member films would take about 0.55 s
a page and 22 s a walk; 50,000 about 1.4 s a page and 2.3 min a walk. A page stays far under the 30 s timeout in
either case, and the consumer accepts totals up to 1,000,000 (MaxItemsPerQuery). How the fixed part splits between
rebuilding the map and filtering the query by thousands of ids was not measured.

Limits of the measure: films with no metadata (no people, genres, overview or images), so Items pages are lighter than
on a real library while Collections pages are not affected; the consumer side of a sync (reading this route through a
real JellyMesh consumer) was not run; a shared library that also holds federated folders, films in several
collections and several distinct peers were not tried; one machine, Docker VM of 8 CPUs, nothing else loaded.
POST /Collections on stock 12.4 fails when calls overlap ("boxsets.collection" in use), so the script creates them one at a time.
